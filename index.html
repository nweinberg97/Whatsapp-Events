Every club has someone stuck chasing replies

Meet Anthony, president of a new student ocean sustainability club. Planning one panel event means a Google Form for dietary needs, a Sheet for tasks, a Doc for topic ideas and a Facebook event for RSVPs, plus a steady stream of nudges in the group chat to get people to fill them in.

Low response rates

Every hop to another app loses people. Organizers end up guessing headcount, under-ordering food and missing allergies.

Tools that don't talk to each other

Answers are scattered across forms, sheets and docs. Someone copies them back together, requests access and waits on everyone else.

Cluttered chats

Follow-ups bury real conversation, and one person gets stuck as the group's nag.

Why it matters to WhatsAppBy the end of the week Anthony is wondering whether the club should just move to another app. When planning leaves the chat, the conversation follows. The north star metric at risk is daily active users.

Built for teams and clubs

Social and collaborative group chats were segmented by size, then scored against four criteria. Work chats were left out, since those teams tend to live in Slack or Microsoft Teams.

  • Impact: how much the problem hurts this group
  • Scale: how many users have it
  • Frequency: how often it comes up
  • Feasibility: how practical a solution is to build soon

    Borrow the jobs, not the clutter

    Facebook has events, group pages and polls, but so many tools and notifications that it can feel overwhelming.

    Discord has sub-threads and bots that automate tasks. They fit the pain points on paper, but would make WhatsApp feel cluttered and less personal.

    People choose WhatsApp because it's simple, quick and personal. Group Events adds the event and poll jobs teams need while leaving threads and bots out on purpose.

    Shipping it

    Launch in Australia, in English, with teams and clubs as the beta cohort. Expand to other regions and languages once it proves itself.

    1. Plan

      Scope the work with back-end, front-end and design, and recruit beta testers from clubs and teams.

    2. Build the MVP

      Ship the foundations in agile sprints toward a set beta date.

    3. Iterate

      Fold beta feedback into a more mature version, until it's ready for all of Australia.

    4. Evaluate

      Measure against the metrics below and talk to the groups using it.

    5. Scale

      If it works, make the case to leadership for an international rollout.

    North star: daily active usersKeeping planning inside the chat should lift engagement and retention in group chats.

    • Positive ratings from post-event feedback
    • Events created per group
    • Response rates on polls and RSVPs
    • Retention of Australian group chat users
